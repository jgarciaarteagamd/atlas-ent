**Vocal hygiene** is the set of habits that protect your voice and stop it from tiring or becoming hoarse. This page explains how to **look after your voice** every day, with simple relaxation exercises you can do at home. They do not replace speech and language therapy if we have recommended it.

## What are these measures for?

They reduce the effort on your vocal cords and the tension in your neck. They help if you have [functional dysphonia](/en/areas/larynx/muscle-tension-dysphonia/), a tired voice or hoarseness after a cold, and they also help prevent these problems.

They are a **supporting measure**. If you have been hoarse for more than 4 weeks, we need to look at your vocal cords.

## Before you start

- If you have been **hoarse for more than 4 weeks**, book an appointment first: a lesion needs to be ruled out.
- If you use your voice for work, these measures matter especially for you.
- It is normal for **speaking softly** to feel odd at first.
- The exercises should not **hurt** or strain. If they bother you, stop.

## How to do it

### Everyday habits

1. **Do not strain your voice.** Speak at a comfortable pitch, without shouting or raising the volume much.
2. **Avoid talking over noise.** In noisy places, move closer to the person rather than raising your voice.
3. **Take breaks.** Every so often, rest from talking for a few minutes.
4. **Sip water through the day.** It may help keep the vocal cords lubricated.
5. **Do not smoke** or breathe in smoke. Limit alcohol.
6. **Avoid throat clearing.** Swallow or take a sip of water instead.
7. **Use a soft, relaxed voice.** If you are hoarse, speak naturally and quietly, without forcing it. Also avoid effortful whispering.
8. **If your voice tires, rest for a while.** You do not need to stay silent for whole days.

### Relaxation exercises (5-10 minutes)

1. **Abdominal breathing:** sit or stand comfortably with one hand on your stomach. Breathe slowly and deeply through your nose and feel your tummy rise. Repeat about 5 times.
2. **Yawn-sigh:** imitate a big yawn and, at the end, let the air out through your mouth like a sigh. Repeat several times.
3. **Slow reading:** read a short sentence in a low or moderate voice, with pauses and without forcing it.
4. **Gentle neck massage:** with your fingertips, make soft circular movements on the neck and under the jaw for a few seconds. Do not press on the throat itself.

## Routine

| Routine | What to do |
|---|---|
| **How often** | Habits, every day. Exercises, 2-3 times a day for 5-10 minutes |
| **For how long** | A few weeks, until your voice improves and while you work with your therapist |
| **When to stop** | If an exercise hurts or makes the hoarseness worse, stop and contact us |

Always follow the routine your doctor gave you, which may be different.

## Common mistakes

- **Whispering with effort** in the belief that it rests the voice: it also strains the throat.
- **Clearing your throat** often: it irritates the vocal cords.
- **Shouting or talking over background noise:** it makes you strain.
- **Relying on lozenges or sweets** to cure hoarseness: there is no evidence that they do.
- **Carrying on talking a lot with a poor voice.**
- **Letting weeks pass** with hoarseness without seeking advice.

## What is normal and when to seek advice

**It is normal** for:

- Your voice to be more tired at the end of the day.
- It to take several weeks to improve.

**Stop the exercises and seek advice** if:

- The hoarseness, pain or tiredness increases.
- The hoarseness lasts **more than 4 weeks**.
- You notice a lump in the neck, pain on swallowing or unexplained weight loss.

**Call 112 (in Spain) or your local emergency number, or go to A&E,** if you have difficulty breathing, a high-pitched noise when you breathe, or cannot swallow your saliva.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to stay completely silent if I am hoarse?**
No. It is better to talk less, softly, with breaks, than to say nothing for days.

**Is whispering bad?**
If you do it with effort, yes, because it strains the throat. Speak quietly and naturally.

**Does drinking water help?**
It may help keep the vocal cords lubricated. It does not cure hoarseness on its own.

**Do throat sweets and lozenges work?**
They may ease dryness, but there is no evidence that they cure hoarseness.

**How long does it take for my voice to improve?**
It depends on the cause. If it has not improved in 4 weeks, you need an examination.
