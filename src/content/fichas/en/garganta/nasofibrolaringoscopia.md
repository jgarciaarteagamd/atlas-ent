## What is flexible nasendoscopy?

**Flexible nasendoscopy**, also called **nasoendoscopy** or **nasopharyngolaryngoscopy**, is an examination in which we pass a very thin, flexible tube with a tiny camera on the tip through your nose. It lets us see inside your nose, the upper part of your throat and your **vocal cords**. It is done in the clinic and you do not need to stay in hospital.

The picture appears live on a screen, so you can watch it too while we explain what we see. You may also hear it called a nasal endoscopy or flexible laryngoscopy: it is the same test.

## What is it for?

Some areas of the nose and throat cannot be seen with a normal examination using a light. Nasendoscopy lets us look at them when you have, for example:

- **Hoarseness or a change in your voice** lasting more than 4 weeks. This is the most common reason we do the test.
- **A feeling of a lump or something in your throat**, constant throat clearing or a cough with no clear cause.
- **Difficulty or pain when swallowing** that does not improve.
- **A persistently blocked nose**, snoring or suspected large adenoids in children.
- **Suspected reflux** reaching the throat: [Laryngopharyngeal reflux](/en/areas/throat/silent-reflux-laryngopharyngeal/).
- **Follow-up** of a known problem, such as a vocal cord nodule or polyp, or a vocal cord that does not move well.

It lets us see inflammation, nodules, polyps, lesions or problems with how the vocal cords move.

## How should I prepare?

You hardly need to prepare:

- **You do not need to fast.**
- **Tell us** if you take blood thinners, are allergic to any anaesthetic or often have nosebleeds.
- If you **gag easily**, tell us before we start. We can adapt the technique.
- If the test is for **a child**, explain that it is a small camera, not an injection, and that a trusted adult will be with them.

## What happens during the test?

1. **You sit** in the chair with your back supported and your head upright.
2. **We spray a local anaesthetic** (lidocaine) into your nose to make it more comfortable. It tastes bitter and your nose and throat feel slightly numb.
3. **We pass the endoscope** through one nostril and move it forward gently, without forcing it.
4. **We look at your throat and vocal cords.** We will ask you to breathe, say sounds such as "eee" or "aaa" and swallow, so we can see how everything moves.
5. **We remove it.** The whole test usually takes **about 5 minutes**.

If you gag a lot, we can also put a little anaesthetic in your throat. For children we use a **paediatric endoscope**, sized for them. For patients who need follow-up we often record the examination on video so we can compare pictures between visits.

## Does it hurt? Are there any risks?

**It does not usually hurt, but it can be uncomfortable for a few seconds.** It is normal to feel pressure or tickling in your nose, an urge to sneeze, watering eyes or a bit of gagging. This passes as soon as the endoscope is out.

- **Most common:** mild, short-lived discomfort.
- **Uncommon:** a small nosebleed that stops by itself.
- **Very rare:** dizziness or fainting, an allergic reaction to the anaesthetic or a brief spasm in the throat.

It is a safe, widely used test in ENT. Serious complications are very rare.

## Results

We see the images **at the time of the test**, so we usually explain what we find at the end. If you need anything else (another test, a biopsy or treatment), we will talk it through and plan the next steps with you.

## After the test

- You can **go back to normal life**, including work and driving.
- If we sprayed anaesthetic in your throat, **do not drink water or eat for 30 minutes**, until the numb feeling has passed.
- It is normal for your nose to run a little or for mucus to have a trace of blood for a few hours.

## When to seek advice

**Call 112 (in Spain) or your local emergency number** if you have difficulty breathing or feel you are choking.

**Contact us soon** if, after the test:

- Your nosebleed does not stop after pinching the soft part of your nose for **at least 5 minutes**.
- You have a fever or a severe sore throat that is getting worse.
- Your lips or face swell, or you get severe itching or hives.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does flexible nasendoscopy hurt?**
It does not usually hurt. It can be uncomfortable for a few seconds, mainly from pressure in the nose and some gagging, but the anaesthetic spray helps make it more comfortable.

**How long does it take?**
About 5 minutes in total. The camera is in your throat for only a few moments.

**Is it the same as a nasal endoscopy or a laryngoscopy?**
Yes. They are different names for the same test: a thin tube with a camera passed through the nose so we can see the nose, throat and vocal cords.

**Can children have it?**
Yes. We use a thinner paediatric endoscope. With a good explanation and a trusted adult beside them, most children cope well.

**Can I eat or drink afterwards?**
Yes, unless we have put anaesthetic in your throat. In that case, wait 30 minutes before drinking water or eating.

**Can I drive or go to work afterwards?**
Yes. We do not use sedation, so you can return to your normal activities.

**Will I get a copy of the images?**
It depends. For patients who need follow-up we often record a video to compare with later examinations. Ask us if you would like a copy.
