A **reflux diet** means eating slowly, in moderate amounts, not lying down afterwards, and, for a few weeks, **cutting out the foods that most often make reflux worse**. You then bring them back one at a time to find out which ones disagree with you. This page explains how, step by step.

## What are these measures for?

They reduce how often stomach contents come up and the irritation they cause. They are the foundation of treatment for [laryngopharyngeal reflux](/en/areas/throat/silent-reflux-laryngopharyngeal/) and for acid reflux.

The diet is **temporary**, not a lifelong regime. The aim is to identify **your** triggers. According to studies, the measures that help most are **losing weight if you are overweight**, not lying down after eating, raising the head of the bed and not smoking.

## Before you start

- If you are **pregnant**, have **diabetes** or kidney disease, or have had an **eating disorder**, talk to your doctor first.
- **It is not a weight-loss diet.** Do not stop eating: you change what you eat, not how much you need.
- If you have **warning signs** (difficulty swallowing, weight loss, vomiting blood), seek advice before starting.
- It is normal to find it **hard** at first to give up coffee, chocolate or alcohol.

## How to do it

### Daily measures (always)

1. **Eat slowly** and chew well.
2. **Moderate portions.** Several small meals are better than one large one.
3. **Do not lie down** until **2-3 hours** after eating.
4. **Raise the head of the bed** by about 15 cm, using blocks under the legs at the head end or a wedge. Piling up pillows is not the same: it bends the body.
5. **Loose clothing** that does not squeeze the abdomen.
6. **Do not smoke.** Limit alcohol.
7. **Lose weight** if you are overweight, with your doctor's help.
8. Find ways to **reduce stress**: walks, breathing, relaxation.

### Temporary diet, in three phases

**Phase 1 · Removal (2-4 weeks).** Stop having:

- **Coffee** (including decaf) and drinks with **caffeine**.
- **Chocolate** and mint.
- **Alcohol** and **fizzy drinks**.
- **Fried** and very **fatty** foods (processed meats, rich sauces, heavy stews).
- **Spicy** food and strong seasonings.
- **Citrus fruit and tomato** (fruit, juices, sauce).

Instead, eat: **vegetables**, **non-acidic fruit** (banana, melon, apple…), **wholegrain cereals**, **pulses**, **fish**, **lean meat** and **olive oil** in moderation. Cook **steamed, boiled, baked or grilled**, not fried.

**Phase 2 · Reintroduction.** After those weeks, and if you have improved, **add back one food every 3-4 days**, in a small amount. If symptoms return, make a note: it is a trigger for you. If nothing happens, you can eat it again.

**Phase 3 · Maintenance.** Avoid only the foods that **you** react to. You do not have to give up the rest.

## Routine

| Routine | What to do |
|---|---|
| **How often** | Daily measures, every day. Phase 1 diet, at every meal |
| **For how long** | Phase 1: 2-4 weeks. Then reintroduce one food every 3-4 days |
| **When to stop** | When you finish the removal phase. Do not keep to Phase 1 indefinitely |

Always follow the routine your doctor gave you, which may be different.

## Common mistakes

- **Staying on the restrictive diet for months**, without reintroducing anything.
- **Removing many foods at once** and not knowing which was the problem.
- **Lying down straight after dinner.**
- **Using only pillows** to raise your head.
- **Taking reflux medicine on your own** for months.
- **Skipping meals** or eating less out of fear of symptoms.
- **Carrying on smoking.**

## What is normal and when to seek advice

**It is normal** to:

- Take **2-4 weeks** to feel an improvement.
- Have more symptoms on some days.
- Find it hard to change habits at first.

**Seek advice if:**

- You notice **no improvement** after 4 weeks.
- You lose **weight** without trying or have **progressive difficulty swallowing**.
- **Hoarseness** lasts more than 4 weeks.
- You find yourself afraid of eating or with obsessive thoughts about food.

**Call 112 (in Spain) or your local emergency number, or go to A&E,** if you have tight chest pain, vomit blood or have black stools, or you choke and cannot breathe.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to give up coffee for good?**
No. You remove it for a few weeks and then try it again. If it does not upset you, you can have it in moderation.

**Is decaf coffee allowed?**
In Phase 1, better not. Afterwards you can try it like the others.

**Does alkaline water or bicarbonate of soda help?**
We have no firm evidence that they do. One study used alkaline water together with a plant-based diet, but it was retrospective. We do not recommend it as a treatment.

**Can I eat tomato or citrus fruit if they do not bother me?**
Yes. You only need to avoid what makes things worse for you.

**How long until I feel better?**
Usually within 2-4 weeks, if done properly.

**Does raising the bed help if I sleep on my left side?**
It helps anyway. Sleeping on your left side may reduce night-time reflux in some people.
