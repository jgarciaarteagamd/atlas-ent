A **middle ear infection**, or **otitis media**, is inflammation of the **middle ear**, the air-filled space behind the eardrum. It is very common in young children. There are two main types: **acute otitis media**, with pain and infection, and **glue ear**, also called **serous otitis** or **otitis media with effusion**, in which fluid is left behind without infection.

## What are the symptoms?

**Acute otitis media:**

- Ear pain, often at night and after a cold.
- A temperature, irritability and, in babies, crying and pulling at the ear.
- Sometimes discharge from a burst eardrum. The pain usually eases then.

**Glue ear:**

- Hearing less well: turning up the television or not responding when called.
- A blocked feeling in the ear.
- Usually no pain or fever, so it can go unnoticed.

## Why does it happen?

The **Eustachian tube** links the ear to the nose and ventilates the middle ear. In children it is shorter and works less well during colds. Enlarged **adenoids** can also block it.

The risk is higher with:

- Nursery or daycare.
- Tobacco smoke.
- Prolonged dummy use.
- Bottle-feeding while the baby is lying flat.
- Allergies.

## How is it diagnosed?

We look at the eardrum with an otoscope. Sometimes we measure how it moves with **tympanometry**, a quick, painless test. If fluid lasts for months, we do a **hearing test**.

## How is it treated?

**Acute otitis media:**

- **Pain relief** with paracetamol or ibuprofen is the mainstay of treatment.
- **Antibiotics:** not always needed. We mainly use them in these situations:
  - Children under 2 with infection in both ears.
  - Severe pain, or pain lasting more than 48 hours.
  - A high temperature.
  - Discharge from the ear.
  - Risk factors.
- In mild cases in older children, we can **watch for 48-72 hours** and start antibiotics if there is no improvement.

**Glue ear:**

- The fluid often clears by itself, so we **watch it for about 3 months**.
- Cough and cold syrups, antihistamines and antibiotics have not been shown to help.
- **Autoinflation**, which means blowing up a special balloon with your nose, may help some children a little while we wait. We explain it in [exercises to unblock your ears](/en/areas/ear/unblock-ears-exercises/).
- If the fluid persists and your child hears poorly or has speech problems, we consider **grommets** (ventilation tubes) and, sometimes, removing the adenoids.

**In adults,** glue ear that does not clear needs a full examination of the nose and throat. In adults with persistent glue ear **we also examine the nasopharynx**, the area at the back of the nose, with a thin camera, to rule out anything blocking the Eustachian tube.

## Advice

- Do not allow smoking at home or in the car.
- Breastfeeding and the routine vaccinations help prevent ear infections.
- Do not bottle-feed a baby lying completely flat.
- To clear the nose, we prefer **continuous-mist seawater sprays**. Avoid **syringe rinses**: in clinic we have seen them bring on middle ear infections, probably because they inflame the opening of the Eustachian tube at the back of the nose.
- If your child has fluid in the ears, talk close to them, facing them and without background noise.
- If they have grommets, follow [dry ear care](/en/areas/ear/dry-ear-care/) only if we advise it.

## When should I seek urgent help?

Go to A&E, or call 112 (in Spain) or your local emergency number, if:

- There is **swelling, redness or pain behind the ear,** or the ear sticks out forwards.
- There is a high temperature that does not come down, marked drowsiness, a stiff neck or repeated vomiting.
- The face droops or there is severe dizziness.
- A baby under 6 months has a temperature and ear pain.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does every ear infection need antibiotics?**
No. Many mild infections clear by themselves. We decide according to age, severity and how things progress.

**Does glue ear affect speech?**
It can if it lasts a long time and is in both ears, because the child hears less well. That is why we monitor it and check hearing.

**Can my child bathe with an ear infection?**
If there is no discharge, yes. If the ear is discharging or the eardrum has a hole, keep it out of the water.

**Are grommets a big operation?**
No. It is a short procedure, with a general anaesthetic in children. The tubes usually fall out by themselves after some months.

**Do adults get middle ear infections too?**
Yes, although less often. It can be linked to [Eustachian tube problems](/en/areas/ear/eustachian-tube-dysfunction/), allergies or colds. If the fluid does not clear, we also examine the nasopharynx.
