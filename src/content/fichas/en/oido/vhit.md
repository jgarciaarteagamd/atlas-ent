## What is a vHIT?

The **vHIT** (**video head impulse test**) is a test of the balance part of your inner ear. You wear light goggles with a small camera and, while you look at a fixed dot, we turn your head with small, quick movements. The camera checks whether your eyes stay still on the dot.

When your head moves, the inner ear tells your eyes to move the opposite way, so your gaze stays steady. This is the **vestibulo-ocular reflex**. If part of the inner ear is not working well, the eyes lag behind and have to make a small jump to get back to the dot. The vHIT measures this reflex and picks up those jumps, which are often too quick to see with the naked eye.

Each inner ear has three **semicircular canals**, small tubes that sense head turns. The vHIT can test all six separately.

## What is it for?

We ask for it when you have **vertigo, dizziness or unsteadiness** and want to know whether the inner ear is involved, which side and which part. For example:

- **Severe vertigo lasting days**, as in **vestibular neuritis** (inflammation of the balance nerve).
- **Attacks of vertigo with hearing loss or tinnitus**, as in Ménière's disease, together with [Pure-tone audiometry](/en/areas/ear/pure-tone-audiometry-hearing-test/).
- **Unsteadiness when walking**, especially in the dark or on uneven ground, or **a feeling that things "bounce"** when you move your head.
- **After treatments that can damage the inner ear**, such as some antibiotics.
- **Follow-up** of a known balance problem, to see how it is recovering.

A **normal vHIT is also useful**. In [Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV)](/en/areas/ear/benign-paroxysmal-positional-vertigo/) and [Vestibular migraine](/en/areas/ear/vestibular-migraine-dizziness/) it is usually normal, which helps point to the diagnosis.

## How should I prepare?

Very little preparation is needed:

- **Do not wear eye make-up** (mascara or eyeliner). The camera tracks your pupil and make-up can confuse it.
- **Contact lenses are fine.** Glasses need to come off during the test.
- If you take **medicines for dizziness or sedatives**, tell us when you book. Sometimes it is better to stop them a few days before, but **do not stop them on your own**.
- **Tell us about any neck problems**: severe pain, a recent injury or operation, or a condition of the spine in your neck.
- Wear **comfortable clothes**. You do not need to fast.

## What happens during the test?

1. **You sit** facing a wall, about one to one and a half metres away, with a dot at eye level.
2. **We fit the goggles** snugly so they do not move with your head.
3. **We calibrate the camera.** You follow some dots of light with your eyes, keeping your head still.
4. **We do the head impulses.** We stand behind you, hold your head and turn it suddenly, only a little and at moments you do not expect. Your only job is to **keep looking at the dot without blinking** and keep your neck relaxed.
5. **We repeat** this about 20 times to each side, first horizontally and, if needed, diagonally to test the other canals.

The test takes **10 to 20 minutes**, depending on how many canals we need to test.

## Does it hurt? Are there any risks?

**It does not hurt.** The head turns are small and quick. Some people feel **brief dizziness** or mild neck discomfort, which passes within minutes. If you have a significant neck problem, we assess it first and, if needed, adapt the test or do not do it.

## Results

The machine gives the result **straight away**, with two main measurements for each canal:

- **Gain**: compares how much your eyes move with how much your head moves. When the reflex works well, they move almost the same amount and the gain is **close to 1**. If the inner ear on that side is not working well, the gain **drops**.
- **Corrective saccades**: the small jumps your eyes make to get back to the dot when the reflex is not enough. If they are present, especially if they repeat in the same way, they point to a **weakness** of that canal.

An abnormal result is not a diagnosis on its own. We interpret it together with your symptoms, the examination and other tests, such as a hearing test or videonystagmography.

## After the test

You can carry on as normal and **drive**, unless you feel dizzy; if so, wait until it passes.

## When to seek advice

**Call 112 (in Spain) or your local emergency number, or go to A&E**, if vertigo starts suddenly together with **difficulty speaking, double vision, weakness or tingling** on one side of the body, **a very severe headache** or difficulty walking that is more than just dizziness. These can be signs of a brain problem that needs urgent care.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the vHIT hurt?**
No. You will only feel small, quick turns of your head. Some people feel dizzy for a moment.

**How long does it take?**
10 to 20 minutes, depending on how many canals we need to test.

**Can I go on my own and drive afterwards?**
Yes. If you feel dizzy at the end, wait a few minutes before driving.

**Is it the same as videonystagmography or the water test?**
No. They complement each other. The vHIT tests how the inner ear responds to quick head movements, like those of everyday life. **Caloric tests** (warm and cool water or air in the ear) test the response to slow stimuli. Sometimes one is normal and the other is not, as often happens in Ménière's disease.

**My vHIT is normal but I am still dizzy. Why?**
Because many causes of dizziness do not affect this reflex, such as BPPV, vestibular migraine, or some dizziness related to anxiety or blood pressure. A normal vHIT helps rule out some causes and points towards others.

**Can children have it?**
Yes, if they can cooperate enough to look at the dot and keep their neck relaxed.
