A **temporomandibular disorder (TMD)**, also called a **TMJ disorder**, affects the joint that connects the jaw to the skull, just in front of the ear, and the chewing muscles. It causes **jaw pain, clicking and, quite often, ear pain or a blocked feeling with no infection**. It was once known as **Costen's syndrome**.

## What is a TMJ disorder (Costen's syndrome)?

The **TMJ** is the joint that lets you open and close your mouth, chew and speak. It lies right **in front of the ear**. When the joint or the muscles that move it are overloaded or inflamed, we call it a **temporomandibular disorder**.

"Costen's syndrome" is a **historical** name. In 1934 an ear, nose and throat surgeon, Dr Costen, linked ear symptoms with a poor bite. That explanation is no longer accepted as it stood. We now know the problem is usually **muscular and related to how the joint is used**, rather than the bite.

It is common: it affects between **6 and 9 %** of adults, mainly women between 20 and 50.

## What are the symptoms?

- **Pain** in the jaw, temple or in front of the ear, worse when **chewing** or talking.
- **Clicking, popping** or grating noises when opening and closing the mouth.
- **Difficulty opening** the mouth fully, or a feeling that it "catches".
- **Headache** at the temple and neck tension.
- **Ear pain** without infection, a **blocked-ear feeling** or **tinnitus** (ringing).
- Sometimes **dizziness** or unsteadiness.

Ear symptoms are common: in one review of studies, **more than half** of the adults with a TMJ disorder had ear pain and tinnitus, and an even larger proportion had a blocked-ear feeling.

## Why does my ear hurt if the problem is in my jaw?

The TMJ lies **next to the ear canal**. They share nearby nerves: the **auriculotemporal nerve**, a branch of the trigeminal nerve, supplies the joint and part of the ear canal and eardrum. That is why jaw pain can be **felt in the ear**. The muscles that move the jaw also influence the blocked-ear feeling.

This is what we call **referred pain**: the brain reads as ear pain what comes from the joint and muscles.

That is why it is important for **an ENT specialist to check your ear** and throat before putting the symptoms down to the TMJ, because other causes can also give ear pain.

## Why does it happen?

There is no single cause. Several things add up:

- **Clenching or grinding the teeth** (**bruxism**), by day or at night.
- **Stress** and muscle tension.
- **Habits:** biting nails, chewing gum a lot, resting your chin on your hand.
- **Blows** to the jaw or opening the mouth very wide (for example, a big yawn or at the dentist).
- Arthritis or problems with the disc in the joint.
- Poor posture of the neck and shoulders.

## How is it diagnosed?

Diagnosis is based on your history and an **examination**:

1. **Questions** about pain, noises, habits and stress.
2. **Feeling** the muscles and the joint, and measuring **how far you can open your mouth**.
3. **Examination of the ear, nose and throat**, to rule out other causes of pain or fullness.
4. **[Tympanometry](/en/areas/ear/tympanometry-middle-ear-test/)** or a **[hearing test](/en/areas/ear/pure-tone-audiometry-hearing-test/)** if you notice poorer hearing or have tinnitus.

International diagnostic criteria (**DC/TMD**) are used. **Scans** (MRI or CT) are requested only if there are doubts or the picture is not typical. They are not needed to start treatment.

## How is it treated?

Most people improve with **simple, reversible treatments**. The latest international guideline, for pain lasting **more than three months**, strongly recommends:

- **Education and self-care:** understanding the problem, reassurance and exercises at home. We explain this in [TMJ self-care: exercises and advice](/en/areas/ear/tmj-self-care-jaw-exercises/).
- **Jaw and posture exercises** guided by a professional.
- **Manual therapy** (mobilisation, massage of tension points).
- **Cognitive behavioural therapy** to manage stress and pain.

Painkillers can be used for a short time, as your doctor advises.

The same guideline **does not recommend** other options: **occlusal splints**, injections, **arthrocentesis** and joint surgery. It strongly advises against **irreversible splints** and disc surgery. These are decisions taken with a **dentist**. We refer you to a dentist with experience in orofacial pain and, if a more specialised assessment is needed, to a **maxillofacial surgeon**.

## Advice

- **Do not put anything in your ear** to ease the pain.
- **Avoid chewing gum** and very hard foods while it hurts.
- **Relax your jaw:** lips together, teeth apart.
- **Do not open your mouth to the maximum** when yawning: gently support your chin.
- **Apply moist heat** to the area.
- **Take breaks** if you work at a computer and look after your posture.
- **Book an appointment** if the pain limits you or lasts weeks.

## When should I seek urgent help?

**Go to A&E or call 112 (in Spain) or your local emergency number** if:

- You have **sudden hearing loss**.
- Your mouth **locks** and you cannot open or close it.
- There is **swelling, fever** or redness in front of the ear.
- You are **over 50** and have a **new headache** at the temple, with pain on chewing or vision problems.
- You notice a drooping face.

**Book an appointment soon** if the ear pain does not improve with these measures, if you have new tinnitus or if you cannot eat normally.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a jaw problem cause ear pain?**
Yes. The joint and the ear share nearby nerves, and jaw pain can be felt in the ear.

**Is it serious?**
Not usually, but it can be very troublesome. Most people improve with treatment.

**Does it go away?**
In many people it improves a great deal. It can return with stress or bruxism.

**Is it the same as Costen's syndrome?**
Yes. It is the old name for a TMJ disorder.

**Do I need a splint?**
Not always. The latest guidelines do not recommend splints as a first option. The dentist decides.

**Do I need an operation?**
Almost never. Surgery is kept for very specific cases.

**Why is my ear ringing?**
It may be due to tension in the jaw muscles, but the ear needs to be checked to rule out other causes.
