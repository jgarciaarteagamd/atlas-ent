## What are they for?

Brandt-Daroff exercises are a series of position changes you can do **at home, on your bed**. They are used for [benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV)](/en/areas/ear/benign-paroxysmal-positional-vertigo/) and have two effects:

- **They help move the crystals** that have slipped into the balance canals.
- **They train the brain** to get used to the movement, so dizziness and unsteadiness gradually decrease.

They are a **complement**. To cure BPPV, the repositioning manoeuvre done in clinic (such as the Epley manoeuvre) is more effective. The exercises are useful when some unsteadiness remains after the manoeuvre, when the manoeuvre cannot be done, or to manage relapses, as long as your doctor has recommended them.

## Before you start

- Only do them **if you have been diagnosed with BPPV**. Not all vertigo is the same.
- Sit in the **middle of a wide bed**, with room to lie down on either side.
- The first few times, **ask someone to be with you**.
- **Feeling dizzy during the exercises is normal.** That is exactly the point: the vertigo should get milder day by day.
- If you have **neck or back problems**, talk to your doctor first.
- When you finish, **stay seated for a few minutes** before standing up, and do not drive straight afterwards.

## Routine

| Item | Instruction |
|---|---|
| **Time in each position** | 30 seconds if you have no vertigo, or 1 minute if it appears |
| **Cycles per session** | 2 full cycles, with 1 minute of rest between them |
| **Sessions** | Twice a day |
| **Duration** | 1 week |

Always follow the routine your doctor has given you, which may differ from this one.

## What is normal and when to seek advice

**It is normal** to:

- feel vertigo or nausea during the first days, especially when lying towards the affected side;
- find the symptoms get milder as the days go by.

**Stop the exercises and seek advice** if:

- you have severe vomiting or the vertigo gets worse;
- you notice no improvement by the end of the week;
- new symptoms appear, such as a severe headache, double vision, difficulty speaking, weakness or tingling on one side of the body, or sudden hearing loss. In that case **call 112 (in Spain)** or your local emergency number, or go to the emergency department.
