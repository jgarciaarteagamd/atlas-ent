You can tell **whether a child hears well and whether their speech is on track** by watching what they do at each age. This guide gives you **hearing and speech signs by age**, what to do if you suspect your child cannot hear well, and how to help at home. Finding hearing loss **early** improves language development. It does not replace an assessment by a doctor.

## What is this guide for?

To give you a **reference** for what most children do at each age and **which signs should make you seek advice**. It cannot diagnose: if you have a doubt, see us even if your child seems to be within the expected range.

Studies show that the **earlier** hearing loss is identified and support begins, the better the language outcomes. That is why **there is no need to wait and see whether they talk**.

## Before you start

- Children **do not all develop at the same pace**. These ages show when **most** children manage something.
- If your child was **born prematurely**, was in intensive care, has a **family history of deafness**, had a **serious infection** (meningitis, infection in pregnancy) or **frequent ear infections**, watch more closely.
- A **normal hearing screen at birth does not rule out** hearing loss that appears later.
- Observe your child **calmly and without background noise**.

## How to do it

### 1. Watch what your child does at each age

| Age | What they usually do |
|---|---|
| **0-3 months** | **Startles** at loud noises. Settles to your voice. Looks at you when you talk |
| **4-6 months** | **Turns their head** towards sounds and voices. Makes sounds ("aah", "ooh") and responds when you talk |
| **6-9 months** | **Babbles** ("ba-ba", "ma-ma"). Responds to their name |
| **12 months** | Says "mama" or "dada" meaningfully. Understands "no". Waves bye-bye |
| **15-18 months** | Says **several words** (3 or more by 18 months). Follows simple instructions without gestures |
| **24 months** | Puts **two words** together ("more water"). Points to body parts and pictures when you name them |
| **30 months** | Says about **50 words** and combines words |
| **3 years** | Holds a simple **conversation**. Asks questions. People outside the family understand them most of the time |

### 2. Warning signs

See us if your child:

- **0-6 months:** does not startle at loud noises, does not settle to your voice, does not turn towards sounds or babble.
- **6-12 months:** does not respond to their name, does not babble with syllables, does not understand "no" or use gestures (pointing, waving).
- **12-18 months:** says no meaningful word or does not use gestures or sounds to ask for things.
- **18-24 months:** says fewer than 3 words, does not put two words together or follow simple instructions.
- **2-3 years:** does not regularly use 2-3 word phrases or follow two-step instructions.
- **Over 3 years:** hardly anyone outside the family understands them, they do not use correct sentences or hold a simple conversation.
- **At any age:** **loses** skills they already had, turns the TV **up very loud** or often says "what?"

### 3. If you suspect they cannot hear well

1. **Do not wait.** Book an appointment with your paediatrician or an ENT specialist **as soon as possible**.
2. **Hearing tests suited to the child's age** are done. Some are done even in sleeping babies and do not hurt.
3. We may check for **fluid in the ear** (glue ear), which is a common and treatable cause of poorer hearing. Read [middle ear infection](/en/areas/ear/middle-ear-infection/).
4. If hearing loss is confirmed, treatment (hearing aids, implants or other options) and **speech and language therapy** support start **as early as possible**.

### 4. How to encourage hearing and speech at home

1. **Talk face to face,** close up and in a natural tone. Let them see your mouth.
2. **Name** what you see and do: "look, the dog", "now we put on our coat".
3. **Read stories and sing** every day.
4. **Take turns:** when they babble or say something, respond and wait for their turn.
5. **Cut background noise:** switch off the TV or radio when you talk to them.
6. **Limit screens:** not recommended before 2 years and, from 2 to 4, no more than an hour a day, and less is better. Screens do not make a child speak.
7. If they use **hearing aids**, make sure they wear them as long as we advise.

## Routine

| Routine | What to do |
|---|---|
| **How often** | Watch your child at every well-child check. Encourage language every day |
| **For how long** | While they grow, especially in the first 3 years |
| **When to seek advice** | As soon as you notice a warning sign, without waiting for the next check |

Always follow the routine your doctor gave you, which may be different.

## Common mistakes

- **"They will talk in time":** waiting months without seeking advice.
- **Assuming they hear well because they turn to loud noises,** to vibrations, or when they see you.
- **Relying only on the newborn screening test.**
- **Letting screens do the talking** instead of you.
- **Shouting** so that they can hear you.
- **Putting everything down to "he is shy"** or "she is bilingual" without ruling out a hearing problem.

## What is normal and when to seek advice

**It is normal** for:

- Every child to have their own pace: some talk earlier, some later.
- Words to get mixed up, and difficult sounds to be mispronounced until 4-5 years.
- Hearing to be poorer during a cold.

**Seek advice soon** if:

- Your child has **any of the warning signs** for their age.
- You notice they **hear less well** after an ear infection, or they have frequent ear infections.
- They **lose** skills they already had.

**Go to A&E** if they suddenly stop hearing, after a blow to the head or with a high fever, stiff neck, repeated vomiting or marked drowsiness.

## Frequently asked questions

**They passed the newborn screening. Can I relax?**
Largely, but not entirely: some hearing loss appears later, from infections or other causes. Watch your child and seek advice if you have any doubts.

**My child hears but does not talk. Is it a hearing problem?**
Not always, but it needs to be ruled out. A child who hears but speaks little may need a language assessment.

**Do ear infections affect hearing?**
They can. Fluid behind the eardrum makes hearing poorer, sometimes for months. That is why we monitor it.

**At what age can a hearing test be done?**
At any age, even in newborns. Tests are adapted to the child's age.

**Do screens delay speech?**
It is recommended to limit them because they do not help children talk: language is learnt by conversation.

**Do I need to repeat the screening?**
If there are risk factors or doubts, the doctor may arrange hearing checks throughout childhood.
