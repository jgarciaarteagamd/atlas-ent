To **prevent vestibular migraine attacks**, the most useful steps are to look after a few lifestyle habits and to find **what triggers them for you**. This page explains how to do this with a diary and simple measures, and when to seek advice. It complements any treatment your doctor prescribes.

## What are these measures for?

Attacks of [vestibular migraine](/en/areas/ear/vestibular-migraine-dizziness/) have **different triggers in different people**. These measures aim to reduce how often and how severe your attacks are and to help you feel more secure.

They are a **supporting measure**. Studies on preventive measures are few and small, so we cannot say that any one works for everyone. But they are safe and improve general health.

## Before you start

- Follow these measures **only if you have already been diagnosed** with vestibular migraine.
- If you have dizziness **different from your usual**, seek advice first.
- It is normal not to **notice any change** at first. Give it a few weeks.
- A notebook or an app to write in is enough.

## How to do it

1. **Keep an attack diary.** Note the date and time, how long it lasted, symptoms, what you ate, how much you slept, stress, the weather and, for women, the day of the menstrual cycle. After a few weeks you will see **patterns**.
2. **Sleep regularly.** Go to bed and get up at similar times, including at weekends. Avoid late nights.
3. **Eat at regular times.** Do not skip meals or fast for long periods. Include protein, complex carbohydrates and healthy fats.
4. **Drink water** through the day, especially in hot weather or after exercise.
5. **Exercise moderately and regularly.** Walking, swimming or cycling, without getting exhausted.
6. **Manage stress.** Deep breathing, relaxation or yoga may help. If anxiety is overwhelming, talking therapy helps.
7. **Watch caffeine and alcohol.** Have caffeine in moderation and at regular times. Notice whether alcohol brings on attacks.
8. **Test your possible triggers.** If your diary shows that something keeps recurring (a food, a light, a smell), reduce it and see if you improve. Do not remove everything at once.
9. **Do not overuse painkillers.** Taking them almost daily can make attacks worse. Talk to your doctor if you need them more than 2-3 days a week.

## Routine

| Routine | What to do |
|---|---|
| **How often** | Note every attack; habits, every day |
| **For how long** | Diary for 8-12 weeks; habits, ongoing |
| **When to stop** | The diary, once you know your triggers and are stable. The habits, never |

Always follow the routine your doctor gave you, which may be different.

## Common mistakes

- **Banning many foods at once** without checking whether they affect you.
- **Thinking there is a single diet** that suits everyone.
- **Stopping caffeine suddenly:** it can cause headache.
- **Taking painkillers daily** to avoid attacks.
- **Doing exhausting exercise** only occasionally.
- **Writing nothing down** and relying on memory.

## What is normal and when to seek advice

**It is normal** to:

- Take weeks or months to see improvement.
- Have an attack some days despite looking after yourself.
- Find that your triggers change over time.

**Seek advice if:**

- Attacks are **more frequent** or more severe.
- A **different** kind of dizziness appears.
- You need **painkillers almost every day**.
- Anxiety or fear of attacks limits you.

**Call 112 (in Spain) or your local emergency number, or go to A&E,** if the dizziness comes with a very severe sudden headache, double vision, difficulty speaking, weakness or tingling on one side of the body, or sudden hearing loss.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to give up chocolate and cheese?**
Not necessarily. Only if your diary shows that they bring on attacks.

**Is coffee bad?**
In moderation and at regular times it is usually fine. Stopping suddenly can cause a headache.

**Does exercise help?**
Yes, if it is moderate and regular. Very intense effort can trigger attacks in some people.

**Does my period affect it?**
It can. That is why it helps to note the cycle day in your diary.

**Can attacks be prevented completely?**
Not always, but many people reduce their attacks with habits and, if needed, preventive medication.

**Do any supplements help?**
There is no firm evidence that they work in vestibular migraine. Check with your doctor before taking any.
