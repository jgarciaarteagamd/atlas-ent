Keeping your **ears dry** is part of the treatment if you have [swimmer's ear](/en/areas/ear/swimmers-ear/), an ear infection with discharge or a hole in the eardrum. This page explains how to protect your ear from water in the shower in a simple and safe way.

## What is this care for?

Water that stays in the ear canal softens the skin and encourages infection. Keeping it out helps the drops work and prevents relapses.

## Before you start

- Get large cotton wool balls, **petroleum jelly** and, if you can, a shower cap.
- Wash your hands.
- If you have a **wick** or grommets, also follow our specific advice.

## How to do it

1. Take a cotton wool ball slightly larger than the entrance to your ear.
2. Coat the outside with a thin layer of petroleum jelly.
3. Place it in the **bowl of the ear** (the hollow part), without pushing it into the canal.
4. Put on a shower cap.
5. Wash your hair last, tilting your head towards the healthy side.
6. Remove the cotton wool and throw it away.
7. Dry the outer ear with a towel and tilt your head to let any water run out.
8. If you wish, use a hairdryer on its lowest setting, about 30 cm away, for a few seconds. It **may help**, although studies have not proved it.

## Routine

| Routine | What to do |
|---|---|
| **How often** | At every shower or hair wash |
| **For how long** | While you are on drops, **usually 5 to 7 days**, or as we advise |
| **When to stop** | When we confirm the ear has healed |

**Swimming pools, rivers and the sea:** avoid them **only while you are being treated**. Once the ear has healed, you can go back to swimming.

Always follow the routine your doctor gave you, which may be different.

## Common mistakes

- Pushing the cotton wool into the canal: it can get stuck.
- Using a small piece of cotton wool or none of the jelly: it soaks through.
- Using cotton buds after the shower.
- Covering the ear all day: it needs air.
- Using earplugs that do not fit or are not cleaned.
- Using ear drops without a prescription.

## What is normal and when to seek advice

It is normal to feel some itching or a blocked ear while it heals.

Seek advice soon if:

- Fluid keeps coming out after several days of treatment.
- The pain gets worse.
- The ear bleeds or you feel dizzy.
- The ear sticks out forwards or the area behind it swells.
- You have a temperature, or you have diabetes and the pain is severe.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use earplugs instead of cotton wool?**
Yes, if they fit well and you clean them after each use. Mouldable silicone or custom-made ones seal better.

**My child has grommets: can they get wet?**
Many children with grommets can shower and even swim at the surface without special protection. We will tell you at the check-up.

**Do I have to stop swimming for good?**
No. This care is only for the period of treatment.
