## What is tympanometry?

**Tympanometry** is a quick test that shows how your eardrum moves and how your middle ear is working. The full name of the test is **impedance audiometry**, and it combines two studies: tympanometry itself (the so-called **Jerger curves**) and the **acoustic (stapedial) reflexes**. In most cases we only do the tympanometry.

We place a small probe at the entrance of your ear canal, gently change the air pressure and play a sound. The machine measures how much sound bounces back from the eardrum and draws a **curve** that tells us whether the middle ear is working normally.

## What is it for?

- **Finding fluid behind the eardrum**, known as otitis media with effusion or "glue ear", which is common in children: [Middle ear infection](/en/areas/ear/middle-ear-infection/).
- **Assessing the Eustachian tube**, the channel that ventilates the middle ear, when you have a blocked-ear feeling or pressure: [Eustachian tube dysfunction](/en/areas/ear/eustachian-tube-dysfunction/).
- **Explaining a conductive hearing loss**, together with [Pure-tone audiometry](/en/areas/ear/pure-tone-audiometry-hearing-test/).
- **Clearing up doubts after otoscopy.** Looking at the eardrum is sometimes not enough to tell whether there is fluid, and tympanometry confirms it.

## When do we also measure the acoustic reflexes?

The **stapedial reflex** is an automatic contraction of a tiny muscle in the middle ear, the **stapedius muscle**, when we hear a loud sound. Measuring it gives us information about the middle ear, the facial nerve and the hearing pathway.

In our clinic we keep it for specific cases, such as **facial palsy** or suspected **otosclerosis** (stiffness of the tiny bones of the ear that causes hearing loss).

## How should I prepare?

You do not need any preparation.

- **Tell us** if you have a cold, earache or discharge from the ear.
- If you have **ear tubes (grommets)** or know you have **a perforated eardrum**, tell us (see below).
- If there is a **plug of earwax**, it will need to be removed first.
- For **children**, it helps if they stay still and calm. Crying or talking during the test can change the curve.

## What happens during the test?

1. **We look in your ear** to check that the canal is clear.
2. **We place a rubber probe** that seals the entrance of the canal.
3. **You will feel slight pressure**, like going up in a plane, and hear a soft sound. Stay still: do not talk, swallow or move.
4. **A curve appears on the screen** within seconds. We repeat it in the other ear.
5. **If we also test the acoustic reflexes**, you will hear louder, brief beeps through the same probe while we record the response.

It takes **about 5 minutes** if we only do tympanometry and **about 10 minutes** if we add the reflexes.

## Does it hurt? Are there any risks?

**It does not hurt.** You will only feel pressure in your ear, like in a lift or a plane, for a few seconds. The reflex sounds are loud but very brief. If you are very sensitive to sounds, tell us. There are no significant risks.

## Results

The curve appears **straight away**. The most common types are:

| Curve | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| **Type A** (curve with a central peak) | The eardrum and middle ear move normally. |
| **Type B** (flat curve, no peak) | Usually means fluid behind the eardrum or an eardrum that hardly moves. |
| **Type C** (peak shifted towards negative pressure) | Negative pressure in the middle ear, typical of a poorly working Eustachian tube, for example during a cold. |
| **Type A with a very low or very high peak** | A stiffer (low peak) or looser (high peak) eardrum or chain of tiny bones. |

If we measure the reflexes and they are **absent**, it may be due to a problem in the middle ear, the facial nerve or the hearing pathway. That is why we always interpret them together with the other tests.

A single result is not a diagnosis: we assess it together with the examination and the hearing test.

## When can it not be done?

**It is not done if there is a perforated eardrum or ear tubes (grommets)**. In these situations the results are unreliable and do not change what we decide to do. If the canal is blocked by wax, it needs to be cleaned first.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are tympanometry and impedance audiometry the same thing?**
Almost. Impedance audiometry is the full name of the test and includes tympanometry (the Jerger curves) and the acoustic reflexes. When people say "tympanometry" they usually mean only the first part.

**Does it hurt?**
No. You will only feel slight pressure in your ear for a few seconds.

**How long does it take?**
About 5 minutes if it is tympanometry alone, and about 10 if the acoustic reflexes are added.

**Is it the same as a hearing test?**
No. [Pure-tone audiometry](/en/areas/ear/pure-tone-audiometry-hearing-test/) measures how well you hear. Tympanometry assesses how the eardrum and middle ear work. They complement each other.

**Why have I not had the reflexes measured?**
Because we only do them when they add something, for example with facial palsy or suspected otosclerosis. In most cases the curve is enough.

**Can children have it?**
Yes, as long as they stay still for a few seconds.

**What if I have ear tubes or a perforated eardrum?**
The test is not done in that case, because it does not give reliable results or change the treatment. We will examine you in another way.
